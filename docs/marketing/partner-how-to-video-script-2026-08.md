# Partner How-To Video — "You already booked. Now the easy part." (v1.0 draft, 2026-08-05)

**A different animal from the three SEO videos.** Those fish for strangers on YouTube search. This
one is a **utility video for people who have already booked** — it plays on the partner page, in the
partner's confirmation email, and in the text a partner sends the day after booking. Nobody
discovers this video. It has one job: turn a booked customer into a started group order.

That changes the format. The other scripts are b-roll over voiceover. **This one is a screen
recording with Allan on the front and back end** — because the thing being explained is a screen,
and every second not showing the actual buttons is a second of trust not being built.

**Runtime target: ~1:30.** VO is ~215 words at a relaxed 150 wpm, plus screen-recording beats that
run longer than the words over them. A **:45 cut** for texting is marked at the bottom.

**Written to be partner-swappable** — Premier Party Cruises is the first and biggest use case, but
the same cut works for Airbnb hosts, bach planners, marinas, and bartenders. See "The partner-name
problem" before you shoot; it's the one real production decision in here.

> ⚠ **Status: DRAFT — 3 claims need Allan's sign-off before shooting.** Tracker at the bottom. The
> big one is "free delivery," which is **not** unconditional across partners and is scripted here in
> a way that may be softer than you want.

## 1. COLD OPEN — 0:00–0:10 · **ALLAN ON CAMERA** (van, warehouse, or dockside)

**The opinion: the booking was the hard part. Say so, and mean it.**

> "So you already booked the boat. Or the house, or the planner. Congratulations — that was the hard
> part. Here's the easy part, and it takes about ninety seconds."

*On-screen:* "The hard part: done." · *B-roll cut on "the easy part":* a stocked cooler being set
down on a dock.

*Direction note: this is the whole reason the video works. The viewer just spent real money and is
now bracing for another chore. Open by telling them it isn't one.*

---

## 2. THE PROBLEM — 0:10–0:26 · VO over b-roll

**The opinion: name the misery they're picturing, then take it away.**

> "Because you know what happens next. Somebody has to figure out what twelve people want to drink.
> Somebody has to front the money and chase everybody down for it. And somebody — usually you — ends
> up hauling a cooler through a parking lot in August. You don't have to do any of that."

*On-screen:* three crossed-out lines appearing in time — "Guess what everyone wants" · "Front the
money" · "Haul the cooler." · *B-roll:* NEED — someone struggling with a cooler in a hot parking lot
(shoot it a little comic, not sad); a phone with a wall of unanswered group-chat messages (prop
shot, reuse the bach video's chaotic-group-chat setup if it exists).

## 3. STEP ONE — "Click your link, hit Start Order" · 0:26–0:44 · **SCREEN RECORDING**

**The opinion: they already have the link. Don't send them to a homepage to find it.**

> "One. Click the link you got when you booked. It takes you to a page that's already set up for your
> group — your boat, your rental, your date. Hit *Start Order*. That's the whole first step."

*On-screen recording:* a real phone screen — the confirmation text/email → tap the link → the
co-branded partner page loads with the partner's logo visible → tap the order button. **Record on a
phone, vertical-safe framing**, because that is where this actually gets watched.

> ⚠ **Button-name check before you record.** The generic partner pages say **"Start an Order."** The
> Premier page says **"Order Your Drinks."** If the VO says "hit Start Order" over a Premier
> recording, the words don't match the screen. Pick one before the shoot: either say "hit the big
> yellow button" (works everywhere, weaker), or record per-partner takes, or change the Premier
> button copy to match. See claim #2.

## 4. STEP TWO — "Share the link with your group" · 0:44–1:02 · **SCREEN RECORDING**

**The opinion: this is the feature nobody else has. Slow down and let it land.**

> "Two. You get a link. Send it to your group — text it, drop it in the group chat, whatever you
> already use. Everybody opens it and adds what *they* want. Not what you guessed they want."

*On-screen recording:* the share modal → copy link → paste into a text thread → then a second phone
showing someone else opening it and adding items to their own tab. **Two devices side by side is
worth the extra setup** — it's the clearest way to show that this is genuinely multiplayer.

*On-screen text:* "One link. Everybody adds their own."

## 5. STEP THREE — "Everyone pays for themselves" · 1:02–1:18 · **SCREEN RECORDING → b-roll**

**The opinion: this is the actual objection-killer. Everything else is convenience; this is money.**

> "Three. Everybody checks out separately and pays for their own stuff. You're not fronting anything,
> you're not collecting Venmos, and you're not the group treasurer. It still comes to us as one
> order, and it all shows up together — iced, in a cooler, with your name on it."

*On-screen recording:* a checkout screen, then cut to *B-roll:* HAVE — cooler with a name label on
it (the reviews mention this by name, it's a real signature detail); ice being poured; marina
handoff. NEED — the moment a group walks up and the cooler is already sitting there waiting.

*On-screen text:* "Everyone pays their own. One delivery."

## 6. CLOSE — 1:18–1:30 · **ALLAN ON CAMERA**

**The opinion: end on the shortest possible next action, and a real phone number.**

> "That's it. Drink planning: done. So go click that link — and if you get stuck anywhere, text me.
> That's a real number and it's me on the other end. Party on."

*On-screen:* the partner's link/QR + "Text us: 737-371-9700" held for a full 4 seconds. · *B-roll
cut:* van door closing, or the boat pulling out.

## The partner-name problem — decide before you shoot

Allan's original narration names Premier. The script above deliberately says **"the boat, or the
house, or the planner"** so one cut serves every partner. Three options:

| Option | What it costs | When it's right |
|---|---|---|
| **A. One generic cut** (as scripted) | Nothing — shoot once, use everywhere | Default. Recommended. |
| **B. Generic body, per-partner opens** | Re-shoot only the 10-sec cold open per partner; body and close are shared | Worth it for Premier alone, probably not for the long tail |
| **C. Fully per-partner** | A shoot per partner | Only if a partner is paying for placement |

**Recommendation: A now, add a Premier-specific open (B) if it earns its keep.** Premier is the one
partner with enough volume to justify a second take, and their page already has its own video slot.

## Where this video actually lives

Not a YouTube search play — do not optimize the title for search, and don't expect views to mean
anything. Placements, in order of value:

1. **The partner page itself**, above or beside the order button (Premier's page already has a video
   section — this could replace or sit under the existing boat-day clip).
2. **The partner's booking confirmation email**, if they'll add it — this is the highest-intent
   moment that exists in the whole funnel.
3. **The text a partner sends after booking** — the :45 cut, hosted somewhere that plays inline.
4. Our own post-booking / abandoned-dashboard follow-ups.
5. Unlisted on YouTube as the hosting layer. Unlisted, not public — it has no search job to do.

## Master shot list

*Existing b-roll assumed usable: vans, marina handoffs, warehouse, product close-ups.*

## The :45 cut (for texting)

Keep beats **1, 3, 4, 5** and the last line of **6**. Drop the problem section (§2) entirely and
tighten the close to: *"That's it. Go click that link, text me if you're stuck."* The problem section
is what you lose — acceptable, because a customer being texted this by their own partner is already
past needing to be convinced.
